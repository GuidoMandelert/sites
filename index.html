01

My story

Engineering taught me to look closely. China invited me to look wider.

My background is in mechatronics engineering, a field that brings together mechanical systems, electronics, and intelligent control. It has shaped the way I approach problems: with patience, curiosity, and an interest in the connections between parts.

Coming to China was a decision rooted in that same mindset. I wanted to experience the country beyond what can be understood from a distance and learn more about the culture, society, and everyday rhythms that shape it.

I’m especially interested in how technology and innovation take shape here, and how China’s continuing development may influence the wider world. For me, being here is less about finding quick answers and more about learning how to ask better questions.

02 / Why China?

Learning from a place is different from learning about it.

A

Firsthand perspective

I came to see China as it is lived: through its people, its pace, and the everyday details that do not always travel across borders.

B

Culture and progress

I’m curious about the relationship between a deep cultural history and a fast-moving approach to technology, work, and innovation.

C

A changing world

China’s evolving role in the global economy and technology landscape is one more reason I want to understand it with humility and attention.

03

Engineering & perspective

“The more I learn about systems, the more I value the perspectives behind them.”

Technology is never only about technology.

Mechatronics encourages systems thinking. It asks how mechanical, electronic, and digital elements work together—and how a thoughtful adjustment in one area can affect the whole.

That way of thinking carries beyond engineering. I’m interested in how different cultures define progress, solve problems, and make room for innovation. Learning in China gives me a chance to connect those ideas with a broader understanding of the world around me.

Looking ahead

Still learning. Still looking closer.

I’m here to stay curious, listen carefully, and let new experiences widen the way I see engineering, culture, and possibility.
